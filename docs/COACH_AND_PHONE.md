# The coach, the phone's full capability, rewards and sleep

Part 2 of `docs/MASTER_PLAN.md`. Nothing here is built. It is how Spark uses everything the phone in your hand can do to take executive function off you, in the manner of self-directed CBT: cue, action, immediate reward, honest feedback, and a scaffold that comes down as the habit becomes yours.

It sits on the spine in `docs/LIFE_MODEL.md` (day model, fact bus, context, door pool) and the steps in `docs/DAY_ATLAS.md`.

**What I am not.** I am not a clinician and this is not therapy. The techniques below are standard, evidence-based self-help methods, used the way a workbook uses them. Where a technique can do harm (sleep restriction, especially alongside a stimulant) I have left it out and said so.

## 1. The method, in plain terms

| Technique | What it is | Where Spark uses it | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **If-then plans** (implementation intentions) | "If X happens, then I do Y", fixed in advance | Every step is written as a cue and an action. The coach speaks the cue and the action, never a vague aim. | Small trials in ADHD show gains on executive tasks; they are lab tasks, so I treat it as a strong design principle, not a promise |
| **Behavioural activation** | Schedule small, real activities that reward and give mastery, instead of waiting to feel like it | The day model is all small steps; protected free time and the reward menu are scheduled like work | Core CBT for low mood and low drive |
| **Self-monitoring** | A diary of facts | Sleep diary, step times, phone use and mood are collected automatically; you see numbers, not opinions | Core CBT and CBT-I |
| **Stimulus control** | The bed is for sleep; if you cannot sleep, get up | Docking, the awake list, the cutoff | A first-line part of CBT-I |
| **Anchoring** | A fixed wake time and fixed cues | The wake anchor, the NFC tags, the places | CBT-I and circadian practice |
| **Cognitive restructuring (light)** | Check a hot thought against the facts | The coach shows what you did when you say "I've ruined the week" | Core CBT, done here with your own numbers |
| **Worry time and parking** | Write the worry down, deal with it later | The brain dump and the parking lot | CBT-I and CBT |
| **Urge delay** ("urge surfing") | An urge passes if you wait it out | The 60 second pause before a closed app | Common in addiction and impulse work |
| **Fading prompts** | Prompts shrink as the habit holds | Scaffold levels | Standard in behaviour change |
| **Immediate, varied reward** | Reward soon after the behaviour, and vary it so it stays alive | Earned phone time, the reward menu, the win log | Behavioural principles |

**What I left out:** sleep restriction (cutting time in bed to build sleep drive) is a clinical technique that can make a hard week worse and interacts with stimulants. Spark anchors and protects sleep; it does not restrict it. If you ever want that, it should be with a clinician.

## 2. The coach

The coach is the only part of Spark that speaks. It reads the day model, the context and the facts, and decides one thing at a time: what is the single next useful prompt. It does not decide what is allowed (that is the gate).

### 2.1 Prompt channels, in order of escalation

| Level | Channel | Example | Used when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **A haptic pattern** and a quiet visual | Two short buzzes | The cue for a step that usually goes well |
| 2 | **A spoken line** (text to speech) and the haptic | "It's 07:50. Seat. Bible." | The cue was missed, or it is a Guided step |
| 3 | **A notification with an action** | "Start" and "10 more minutes" (allowed twice) | The step is late |
| 4 | **The gate** | The social list stays closed until the proof | The step is a gate step (M6, K1, N2) |

It never goes past level 4, and it never repeats a level more than once. Prompts never come in a crowd: one voice, one prompt at a time.

### 2.2 Haptic vocabulary (so you can act without looking)

| Pattern | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Two short | Start |
| One long | Stop |
| Long, short, long | Alarm, up |
| Three short | Leave now |
| One soft pulse | Reward |
| Slow double | Goodnight, docked |

### 2.3 The one-line rule

Every prompt is one sentence, in the second person, with the verb first: "Sit in the seat." "Water, then light." "Leave in five." Section 3 of `docs/MANUAL.md` has the voice guide.

### 2.4 Mood check-ins and what the coach does with them

Two optional taps a day, 1 to 5 ("How are you?"), taking about 3 seconds. They are not used to judge you. They are used for two things only: (a) to show you a plain chart in the weekly review, with sleep and work beside it, so you can see patterns yourself; and (b) to notice a run of low days.

If mood is 1 or 2 for three days in a row, or sleep is under 4 hours three nights in a row, Spark does not lecture. It shows one card: *"The last few days have been hard. Do you want a lighter week? You can also talk to someone."* The two actions are **Lite mode** (a door, drawn from the pool) and **People who can help**, a short list you can edit: your GP, the University of Birmingham's Mental Health and Wellbeing Service, Student Disability Service for a reasonable adjustments plan, NHS 111, Samaritans (116 123, free, any time) and Shout (text 85258). The list is information. Spark never diagnoses and never decides for you.

## 4. Rewards: a place where it feels good to build a routine

You asked for it to feel good to use the app. The rewards below are real, immediate, varied, and are never earned by faking.

### 4.1 What you get, and when

| Moment | Reward | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A step is proven | A soft chime and a single haptic pulse, and a tick that draws itself (AN1) | New |
| A block is proven | Earned phone time: 10 minutes open per hour of work (R2), money (R8), the Buffer visibly improves (R1) | Have |
| A routine is complete | A line of what it was, in your words (for the Bible: your three lines are shown back to you next week) | New |
| The day is complete | **Today's wins:** three true things the day shows, picked from the facts. "Up on time. Bible done. Two cycles finished." | New |
| A good night | The morning card: "7 hours 40. Up on time." and a longer override window (N5), and a reward from your menu | Have and new |
| A week | A short highlight: the best streak-free fact ("11 of 14 blocks proven"), the number of nights docked, the money, and one photo you chose | New |

### 4.2 The reward menu

You choose five rewards once (a playlist, a coffee, a walk, a call, an episode, a treat, a gym song). When a block or a routine is proven, Spark offers one, at random from your five. A random choice from your own list keeps the reward fresh and keeps you from gaming it. The reward is always taken in the free time that was earned.

### 4.3 No streaks, no points, no shame

A streak turns one missed day into a failure. Spark shows what you did, never what you broke. Nothing in the app is red for a missed day; it is neutral grey with the next step in front. There is no leaderboard, no level, no badge. The progress rings are drawn from real numbers (CH1).

### 4.4 Identity, in your own words

At setup you write three sentences about who you are trying to be ("I keep my word to myself"; "I look after my body"; "I finish what I start"). The coach quotes them back sparingly, tied to a fact: "That's six promises kept today." It is your language, not mine.

## 5. The sleep programme

You asked how a student who struggles with sleep is encouraged to sleep instead of being on the phone, texting or jumping out to do something fun. The answer is a programme, not a rule, because the pull has a few separate causes.

### 5.1 The pull at night, and the answer to each

| Cause | What it feels like | Spark's answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The day was owed to work, so the night is the only free time** | "I want some time that is mine" (this is called revenge bedtime procrastination) | The free time is **scheduled earlier** and protected (G3), and the evening's fun goes into a slot **tomorrow** (the "tomorrow's fun" step). The pull gets a real answer, not a no. |
| **The phone is a hand-held reward machine** | Pick up, scroll, an hour is gone | The phone is **docked across the room** by tag; the night rule closes the social list after the cutoff; the screen goes grey and warm 45 minutes before |
| **A mind that will not switch off** | Thoughts, plans, worries | The **brain dump** and the parking lot: written, parked, safe |
| **A body clock that runs late** (DSPS) | Not sleepy until 03:00 | A **fixed wake anchor**, morning light, and a **gradual shift** (section 5.3), not a sudden change |
| **A friend texts at 01:00** | Fear of missing out | A prepared reply you can send in one tap ("Going to sleep, free tomorrow at 17:00?"). Spark drafts, you send. |
| **A spark of an idea** | "I must do this now" | The **parking lot**. It is real and it will be there. |
| **A late hyperfocus session** | You cannot stop | A hard stop at the cutoff (N2, N7), a gentle prompt at T minus 20, and the awake list afterward |
| **Alcohol or a late meal** | Wrecks the night | The meal check; a single question the evening before a late night out |
| **Noise or a hot room** | Cannot settle | The awake list has options; nothing to track |

### 5.2 The night in four phases

1. **Evening (free time)**: protected, scheduled, the phone open. This is where the fun is.
2. **Wind-down (T minus 60 to T)**: the routine in `DAY_ATLAS.md` section 4. The screen softens, the brain dump, the tomorrow's-fun step, prayer, stretches, warm milk, docking.
3. **The night**: docked, dark, still. Sleep is measured.
4. **The morning**: the morning card shows last night's number without judgement and sets today's tier.

### 5.3 The gradual shift for a delayed clock

A target wake time that moves 15 minutes earlier every 2 to 3 days until it reaches the target, with a bright-light step on waking and the wind-down moving with it. The existing pull-back (Z3) is the reverse: the wake drifts later by one night, then returns 15 minutes a day. Both are in the same engine (anchors, SA1). Spark never moves it faster than 30 minutes in a day. Weekends keep the anchor within an hour, because lying in fragments sleep and sets the clock back.

### 5.4 Medication and caffeine

Spark has **your own reminders**: you set "melatonin 3mg at 23:00" or "take with food at 08:00", and it logs taken or not taken for you to show a prescriber. It does not recommend doses or times. If you want to know how a medication fits a 02:30 sleep, the person to ask is your prescriber or a pharmacist. Spark can print the log of when you took it next to your sleep for that appointment.

### 5.5 Naps

After a night under 4 hours, Spark offers a 20 minute nap window before 15:00, once, as a Default you can switch off. Longer or later naps work against the night, so they are not offered.

### 5.6 The one hard line

A late night is a normal part of your life, and the plan lets you choose it (anchors, tonight's plan). What the plan does not do is let the wake time stay put while the sleep disappears: lights-out and wake move together, always at least 8 hours apart (SA3), unless you go through the wall.

## 6. The photo, and why you cannot skip past it

You said: "I like the photo idea, but I can easily just skip past it." The design answer is in `LIFE_MODEL.md` section 2.2, summarised here with the specific cases.

| Photo | If you ignore it | Why that is not the easy path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning setup (stops the alarm) | The alarm keeps ringing; no snooze exists (M2) | You cannot ignore a ringing alarm |
| Ready photo | Social stays closed until it is taken (M8) | The phone stays closed to the things you want |
| Work block proof | The block stays open; no earned phone time, no money, the week shows unconfirmed time (R2, R8) | You lose the reward and the number moves the wrong way |
| Attendance photo | It is the only proof (L1) | Without it, the lecture is unproven and the cycle waits |
| Shutdown photo | No penalty for sleep, but the setup earns nothing and the next morning uses the last reference (S1) | The morning gets harder, by your own design |
| Chore photo | The chore stays open; the reward waits | The reward you wanted is on the other side |

**Three ways to make skipping rarer, not just costlier:**

1. **Cheaper proof.** NFC and sensors replace photos wherever they are strong enough: the desk, the bathroom, the charger, the seat, the door. A one second tap beats a photo.
2. **One photo, many uses.** The same photo can prove the setup and become the reference.
3. **Reminders that stop.** Four levels and no more (section 2.1). A nagging app gets deleted.

**Faking:** the match is against your own reference, in the window, at the place (L1 to L3). A photo of an old photo is caught by the match failing on lighting and by the timestamp. A fake that costs more effort than doing the thing is not a loophole; a fake that costs less is, and `docs/COHERENCE_AND_BYPASS.md` lists where I think those still exist.

## 7. The rule for adaptation: "the app adapts to you"

You said: "how does the app then adapt to me?" Four ways, all automatic:

1. **Measured times replace guessed times** (T2, the observe phase).
2. **Prompts fade as habits hold and return when they slip** (scaffold levels).
3. **The day type is proposed from where you are** (context) and confirmed once.
4. **The tier follows the day** (Full, Short, Floor), picked by the yield policy from the time, the sleep and the context.

You supply the three things only you can: what matters (your identity sentences, your reward menu, your places), what happened (a tap when something takes longer, or you are out), and when to change the economy.
