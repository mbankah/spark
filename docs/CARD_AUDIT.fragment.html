How the 100 points are made

Practical utility 20Rated 0 to 5 by the effort, time or mistakes it saves; 5 changes what you do every day. Dropped one point this round unless it was 5.
Unique purpose 15The lower of my rating and what the graph allows: 5 when nothing else is more than 0.4 similar to it, falling to 0 for a twin.
Distance 2020 when no similar card is near. It falls to 0 when a card 0.6 similar is within 1 tap, and a similar card 2 taps away counts 70%, 3 taps 40%, 4 or more nothing.
Moment 10Rated 0 to 5: 5 is time-critical and shows exactly then. Dropped one unless 5.
How often 10Rated 0 to 5: 5 is many times a day, 3 a few times a week. Dropped one unless 5.
Room it takes 5Rated 0 to 5; less room scores higher.
What breaks without it 10Rated 0 to 5: 5 safety, proof or blocking; 4 the Buffer or money. Dropped one when it was 3 or less.
Rule behind it 10A Locked rule names it 10, a Default rule 4, no rule 0.
SimilarityEach card is tagged with its job, its data, its moment and how you use it. Two cards that share no job score 0; sharing a job gives at least half, and the other three raise it to 1.
Verdict 60 / 40Pinned by a Locked rule: keep. In a system: the owner is scored once the others are folded in, and the others merge. Alone: 60 or more keeps; below it, a unique purpose of 0 or 1 (or under 40 with little use) deletes, and anything else moves down.

The ratings are my judgement from reading the code, and the tags behind the graph are too: every tag is in tools/card_graph.py and every number is in its row, so any of them can be overruled and the totals move with it. 5 is exceptional and 3 is ordinary. Rules are named by their ledger id: P is a Locked rule that names the card, A is a Default rule that would be amended in the same change. Widgets, notifications and the lock screen repeat Home facts on purpose (B2) and are not scored as duplicates. The breakdown reads: practical utility, unique purpose, distance, moment, how often, room, what breaks, rule.
